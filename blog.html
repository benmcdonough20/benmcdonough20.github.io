8-3-26: Introduction to higher-form symmetries

Symmetry is the organizing principle of physics. It has recently been appreciated that the notion of an ordinary, or global, symmetry of a field theory admits a massive generalization to higher-form symmetries. Instead of thinking of a symmetry as a transformation of the theory which leaves it unchanged, the framework of higher-form symmetries rephrases symmetry defects as a homological pairing between a higher-form conserved current and a closed submanifold of the same dimension. This typed set of notes based on a presentation I gave to a journal club dives into the foundational ideas of higher-form symmetry and introduces the tools of differential geometry needed to read the literature on this topic.

6-26-26: Quantum error correction and the torsor-cocycle correspondence

The codewords of a quantum double (as explored in my recent paper) correspond to monodromy representations of the fundamental group of the base space into the gauge group, intuitively given by all the ways to drag a magnetic excitation around a non-contractible loop in the complex. However TQFTs correspond to principal G-bundles, so how do these notions relate? I read this fantastic set of blog posts which explain how monodromy representations are connected to principal bundles through the torsor-cocycle correspondence. It turns out that there is a beautiful way to describe quantum double codewords in terms of Cech cocycles with coefficients in the sheaf of locally constant maps into the gauge group. The transition functions of a principal bundle are given by the local surjectivity of the gauging map, and the local kernel of the gauging map corresponds to sections of the projection to the base space.

3-16-26: Symmetry-protected topological states, topological order, and group cohomology

Both symmetry-protected topological phases and their long-range entangled gauged counterparts, twisted quantum doubles, are classified by the cohomology of their symmetry group. Here I try to make this discussion completely transparent and prove some facts about SPT states at the lattice level. I also dive into one of the most studied examples, the type-III twisted double on three copies of a toric code, from several perspectives; gauging an SPT state, gauging a logical symmetry constructed through the cup product, PEM duality, and decorated domain walls.

3-10-26: The double centralizer theorem

The double-centralizer theorem plays an important role in the modern theory of Hilbert space shattering. Here, I lay out the basics of the representation theory of semisimple algebras, prove the double centralizer theorem, and illustrate its application with Shur-Weyl duality. Lastly, I present a method for constructing the irreps of the symmetric group using the machinery of induced representations and Frobenius reciprocity.
